For the last few years, I have been thinking about how to (1) do probabilistic modeling (2) create a simple, relateable example using MCMC such that it would help even a layman gain an understanding of the method's useability. Lately, I realized that I have somewhat achieve (1) (yay!), and it was yesterday that I had a thought about (2).

I live with my friend Daniel, and our living agreement is that I'm the cook and he's the cleaner. After dinners, I sometimes ask myself whether I cook enough to make him go not hungry, and whether my food is any good. I realized that it would be quite easy to obtain the answer through modeling my belief as a Gaussian distribution conditioned on my observations of his (a) comments about said meal (b) leftover, and use MCMC to infer the posterior.

Let's proceed as follow:

Denote probability of Daniel still hungry after dinner as $\mathbb{P}(x\mid a,b)$, with $a\in[-1,1]$ whether he said anything nice about the food (positive values means positive thing), and $b\in[0,1]$ the percentage of food leftover. 

According to Bayesian rule: $\mathbb{P}(x\mid a,b)\propto \mathbb{P}(a,b\mid x)\mathbb{P}(x)$. 

What I want to get out of this is that, given what he said about the food, and how much leftover is there afterward, I want to know whether he is still hungry or not and how much. If the outputted gaussian's means is in the negative, I would consider him hungry. If the outputted gaussian's means is in the positive, I would consider him not hungry. Of course, that mean is obtained through averaging all the samples from $\mathbb{P}(x\mid a,b)$ 

I know that I can use MCMC in order to obtain such samples. 

$$\begin{eqnarray}
\mathbf{E}[\frac{1}{N}\sum^N_{i=1} \frac{f(x_i)}{p_X(x_i)}] &=& \frac{1}{N}\sum^N_{i=1}\mathbf{E}[\frac{f(x_i)}{p_X(x_i)}]      \nonumber \\
&=& \frac{1}{N}\sum^N_{i=1}\int_X \frac{f(x)}{p_X(x)} p_X(x) \\
&=& \frac{1}{N}N\int_X f(x) \\
&=& \int_X f(x) 
\end{eqnarray}$$


Note the [law of the unconscious statistician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_the_unconscious_statistician): $\mathbf{E}_{p_X}[f(x)] = \int_X f(x) p_X(x) dx$
